## The launch pattern (memorize the shape)

```bash
python -m scripts.run \
  --scenario configs/scenarios/<CELL>.yaml \
  --reference-cell <CELLNAME> --seed <SEED>
```

- `--reference-cell` writes to `reference_runs/<scenario>/llm_agent/<CELLNAME>__seed<SEED>/` — a STABLE directory, so a crashed/rate-limited run is resumed by RERUNNING THE IDENTICAL COMMAND: every already-paid response replays from the run-local cache at $0 (Phase 2.7 needed 4 attempts; the cache absorbed the cost). Never launch live with a bare `--out-dir runs` (fresh timestamp = cold cache = re-pay on crash).
- One cell at a time. No parallel live runs (shared rate limits; interleaved spend is unauditable).
- Expect 45–90 min wall per 24 h Haiku cell (Phase 2.8: 64 min for 12 h).

#### Mechanism — measured, after two wrong hypotheses

**Negotiation has a bandwidth cost, and under a per-tick message budget it exceeds its value.**

Delivered messages, comm cell (sent − budget_overflow − comm_drop, from `messages.jsonl`):

| delivered | live LLM | control |
|---|---|---|
| INFORM | **1823** | 1467 |
| OFFER | 417 | **548** |
| REQUEST | 1234 | **2091** |
| ACCEPT + COUNTER + REJECT | **824** | **0** |
| **total** | 4298 | 4106 |

Both arms deliver ~the same total — the budget binds. But the LLM spends **824 messages (19% of its delivered traffic) on negotiation replies the control never sends**, and replies are emitted *first* (`llm_agent.py:299-303`, before `act()`'s OFFER/REQUEST at `:317-326`). So they displace the messages that actually move energy: the control gets **60% more OFFER+REQUEST delivered (2639 vs 1651)**; its OFFER drop rate is **0%** vs the LLM's **40.9%**, and its REQUEST drop rate 30.5% vs 50.9%.

Compounding it, `Commitment` is served in `act()` at `agent.py:766-795` — *before* the below-mean-SoC filter at `:804-823`, and exempt from it by design ("a promise is a promise", `agent.py:184-192`). So the energy the LLM does move is also the least belief-validated. `agent.py:813-814` (`if not peer_fracs: return c_transfers, c_outbox`) is a **full stop for the control** (`c_transfers` is always `[]` — it never creates commitments) and a **pass-through for the LLM**. Same line, opposite behaviour.

**Two hypotheses were refuted along the way; recording both so they are not re-proposed:**
1. *Mine — "expired promises crowd out alternatives."* **No such channel exists.** A `Commitment` lives only in the sender's ledger; recipients never parse ACCEPT (`observe()` queues only REQUESTs, `agent.py:375`), and `_emit_requests` never consults promises (`agent.py:913-936`). A house with an outstanding promise re-requests its full deficit next tick. Expiry also ships nothing and reserves nothing (`agent.py:770-772`), so the 35.2% expiry rate is a *symptom* of over-promising, not a damage channel.
2. *"The LLM starves its own INFORMs with chatter."* **Refuted by the data**: INFORM drop is 80.0% live vs **83.3% control** — the LLM actually gets *more* INFORMs through (1823 vs 1467). It is not belief-starved relative to the control.

**⚠️ Scope of the claim — this is NOT "LLM coordination is fragile."** Send order is hardcoded plumbing the LLM cannot control, and a priority scheme that shipped OFFER/REQUEST before replies under scarcity would likely change the result. The defensible claim is narrower and still interesting: **a negotiation protocol costs bandwidth, and under bandwidth scarcity that overhead can exceed the value of the coordination it buys, leaving a filter-based heuristic ahead.** Do not write this up as evidence about LLM reasoning under communication failure — the cell does not test that.

Follow-up bugs surfaced (for 3.3, not blocking): commitments are appended (`agent.py:704-711`) *before* the bus send (`llm_agent.py:302-303`), so a commitment is created and honoured even when its ACCEPT was dropped as `budget_overflow` — **senders ship energy against promises the recipient never received**. And `agent.py:797-798` returns `c_transfers` even when the sender is below its own `share_min_soc_frac`, i.e. it exports promised energy in the same tick it begs, at ~15% round-trip loss.

Caveats: **n=1 seed.** Replicate before this carries paper weight. Cost $4.03 (< clean's $5.59: starved messages → 70% fewer react calls → a cheaper cell).

#### The doubled commitment-expiry signal — checked, and it is NOT defectors reneging

1. **Defectors defect by ACCEPTing less, not by reneging.** The 4 have-defectors cut their commitments (ACCEPT+COUNTER) from 396 (clean) to 162 (−59%) and their OFFERs collapse (e.g. r0c1 149→49, r4c0 164→64, r3c2 271→115). Prompt-realization works: they *withdraw supply up front*. The 8 honest haves are unchanged (ACCEPT 813→817). Heterogeneity is visible — r0c1 went near-silent (51→6 ACCEPT) while r1c3 barely complied (50→62).
2. **The mechanism forbids reneging.** A `Commitment` is served in `act()` at `agent.py:759-795` — *before* the below-mean-SoC filter at `:797` and exempt from it. The engine ships the promised energy up to physical headroom regardless of the agent's "wishes"; there is no act()-time channel to refuse. A commitment expires (`agent.py:770-772`) only when the sender's deliverable headroom ran dry before its 3-tick TTL.
3. **Attribution rules defectors out numerically.** Have-defectors make only **180 of 2622 commitments (6.9%)**; even if every one expired it would be ≤38% of the 468 expiries. And defectors *hoard* — high SoC means ample headroom to deliver — so their commitments are the *least* likely to expire.

**So the doubled expiry is a scarcity artifact, not defection-by-reneging.** With 33.6% of generation withheld, the honest suppliers' unchanged promise volume more often outruns batteries left depleted by the missing supply → promises can't be fully served within TTL → they expire. This is over-promising under scarcity by the *loyal* houses, the same channel flagged in `agent.py:797-798` (exports while below own threshold), amplified here by the smaller supply pool.

Fairness moves *with* efficiency again (never gini alone): gini 0.2002 and Jain 0.8658 are both healthier than the comm cell and than clean seed-23. `min_house_served_fraction` 0.0418, `served_critical_load_fraction` 0.8805 (non-vacuous). parse failures 10 (< 5% bar).

Caveats: **n=1 seed.** The realized dose is 33.6% (4 of 12 haves) — pinned by `scripts/dose_check.py`; **any second seed will land a different dose and must report it.** Cost $6.07.

### Why they are immune — the mechanism (code trace, 2026-07-16)

`failure_modes` has **exactly one application site in the codebase**: `llm_agent.prepare` (`sim/strategies/llm_agent.py:125-145`), which `llm_fallback` also routes through (`llm_fallback.py:54-64`). Nothing else reads it. `sim/engine.py:235,324` only *serialize* it.

- **round_robin** never touches the message layer: it defines only `decide_transfers`, so `run.py:105` hands it `message_bus=None`, and it reads engine ground-truth SoC directly (`round_robin.py:40,48`). Immune to `comm` (no messages to drop), to `noise` (never reads the perturbed channel), to `defectors` (no prompts).
- **lp_optimal** is an oracle over ground-truth profiles with perfect foresight and explicitly ignores the comm graph (`lp_optimal.py:115-120` + module docstring). Immune by construction.
- **The five YAMLs are physically identical apart from the `failure_modes` block** — so for round_robin and lp_optimal all five configs are literally *the same simulation*. That is why they are bit-identical rather than merely close.
- **control under `comm` (−12.4):** INFORMs are emitted last (`llm_agent.py:331-334`), so `per_tick_budget: 2` starves them (`protocol.py:120-139`); empty `peer_beliefs` then hits the early return at `agent.py:813-814` → **no discretionary sharing at all**. The damage is *eligibility collapse*, not degraded reasoning.
- **control under `defectors` (+0.0) is structural, by design (P3.1 T5):** `use_wrapper=False` makes `maybe_corrupt` the identity, and the selfish system prompts are built but `llm_disabled=True` means no LLM call ever reads them (`agent.py:386,607`).

**⚠️ `summary.json`'s `failure_modes_active` means CONFIGURED, not applied.** A round_robin summary in the comm cell truthfully reports `per_tick_budget: 2` while no comm code ran. Do not read it as evidence of exposure — check the strategy. (Caveat now in `sim/logging.py`'s docstring.)

**⚠️ Do NOT report "noise helps the control".** The +1.3 is one seed's realization luck, not a mechanism. Both channels were measured directly (2026-07-16): the load clamp `max(0, L·(1+0.15z))` binds only at z < −6.67 (p≈1e-11) for a **+0.048%** bias — three orders of magnitude too small to matter. The SoC clamp `clip(S + 0.10·cap·z, 0, cap)` *is* biased (a full 10 kWh battery reads 9.60, an empty one reads 0.40) but it is **mean-reverting**: haves look less full → share less, have-nots look less empty → request less. Both *reduce* sharing, so it could only hurt. Nothing in either channel can systematically help; +1.3 sits well inside the ~16-pt cross-seed spread.

## Failure playbook

- **429 / OAuth 401 mid-run:** wait for the limit window, rerun the IDENTICAL command; cache replays paid calls. Three consecutive 401s → re-mint the token, do not edit code.
- **Crash mid-run:** rerun identical command (stable `--reference-cell` dir). Verify `cache_hits` in the relaunch's summary ≈ calls made before the crash.
- **Numbers look insane** (served < control − 0.05 or > LP): stop spending; run the mock replay of the same cell (`--strategy llm_fallback`) and diff configs; suspect a `--set` typo first (they hard-error now, but check `config.json` provenance in the run dir).
- **A YAML edit is needed mid-campaign:** any prompt/YAML change invalidates cross-cell cache comparability — finish the current stage first, note the change in the log table, never mix architectures within a stage.
